The guide

Everything Orbital does and every setting behind it, in the order the settings themselves are listed. Hold an empty part of the home screen to reach any of it.

Getting started

Set Orbital as your home app and it will offer to fill the dock in from your default apps and what you already open most. Nothing is taken from you to do that — it suggests, you accept.

From there, three gestures open everything: hold an empty part of the background for the home menu, swipe up for the app drawer, and swipe sideways to move between pages.

The orbit dock

The dock is the reason the launcher exists. Apps ride an ellipse along the bottom of the screen; you turn it with a thumb and tap what comes round to the front. It holds fifteen apps on the first orbit, with two more orbits behind it.

Shape and feel

The dock
The orbit, a flat row, or nothing at all — for somebody who wants everything else about Orbital and reaches for apps by swiping up.
Anchored to
The bottom, or either side. Down a side the flat row becomes a column.
Shape
How flat the ellipse is, from a shallow arc to a full circle that shows every slot at once.
Position, size, icon size, icon spacing
Where it sits along its edge, how far it reaches, and how much room each app takes on it.
Sensitivity, snap to slots, snap strength
How far a drag turns it, whether it settles on each app or spins freely, and how hard it is pulled into place.
Keep icons upright
Turn the phone and only the icons turn, if the phone is set to rotate.
Orbit when idle, wind back when idle
The dock can drift slowly by itself when untouched, and return to where it started after a few seconds.
Haptics
A tick each time a slot passes the front, with its own strength.
Names under the icons
Every icon, only the one in front, or none.

What is on it

Orbital's own buttons
A slot that opens the app drawer, and one that opens the settings.
The middle of the orbit
Which app the dock rests on, and where winding back returns to.
The orbits behind the first
One, two or three orbits. The ones behind sit tucked in until you drag outward from an icon; how deliberate that pull has to be, and how far apart the orbits sit, are both yours.
Dynamic dock
Fills the dock with the apps you open most, keeps the outer orbits on the ones you use next, and can put the last three apps you opened on the end. Anything you hold in place stays put. Needs usage access.

Inside other apps

The dock can stay on the screen edge while another app is in front, resting flat until you touch it and climbing back out when you do. It can learn to stay away from apps you keep pushing it out of, and the handle it leaves behind can sit left, centre or right. Needs permission to draw over other apps.

The home screen

Pages are a lattice rather than a strip: you can add one to the left, the right, above or below, and every page can be named. Widgets are placed as fractions of the page, so they keep their arrangement whatever screen they end up on.

Arranging it

Hold the background and choose Edit mode (or pinch, if you would rather). The page steps back, the edges of the page and the dock are drawn faintly so you can see where things will land, and a tap chooses one widget at a time. The chosen one wears a row of chips: lock it, make it smaller or larger, and open its own settings. Drag an edge or a corner to stretch it. Drag it to the bin at the top to take it off.

Settings

Icon size, names under them
How large app shortcuts are drawn on the page, and whether they are labelled.
How the pages are marked
Dots above the dock, named tabs across the screen, or nothing. Tabs can sit at the top or the bottom, and tapping the page's name in edit mode renames it.
Drawer chevron
The little mark above the dock pointing at the swipe up.
Lock the layout
Nothing on your pages moves. New things can still be added.
Line up on a grid, places to land on
Widgets snap into place when you let go, on a grid from coarse to finest. Anything narrower than a cell — the app scroller's rail, for one — can also stand halfway between the lines.
The clock
Face, size, weight, colour, how far down the screen it sits, which way it lines up, what the date under it says, seconds, 24-hour, and a leading zero.

Hold the background and choose Widgets. Orbital draws nineteen kinds itself, and any widget from any app on your phone can go on a page beside them.

Small things worth not opening an app for.

Any Android widget

Everything the phone already has, placed and sized the same way.

Every widget's own settings are a chip away: select it in edit mode and press Settings. Its size and whether it is held in place belong to that widget; anything shared with others of its kind says so on the sheet.

How widgets look

The panel behind a widget is yours: card, bracketed corners, ruled lines, a solid block or nothing at all, with its own corner radius, how solid it is, how heavy its edge, how much of your accent colour is in it, and whether it lifts off the wallpaper with a shadow. Text inside widgets has a size of its own, which no theme touches.

The app drawer

Swipe up anywhere on the home screen. It can slide in from the bottom, the top or either side, and be laid out as a grid or a list with the columns and icon size you want. Down one edge is the alphabet: drag it and the list follows your thumb.

Order
By name, or by what you actually open. Without usage access it counts only the apps you have opened from Orbital.
Recently installed
The newest apps first, above the rest.
The search bar
At the top or the bottom, where it sits above the keyboard. It searches apps and, once you allow contacts, your address book. It can hand a line it cannot place to the assistant.
Hidden apps
Off the list, still found by searching.

The glance hub

One line inside the orbit, cycling through what matters now: how many notifications are waiting and the most recent, what is playing, the next calendar event or note reminder, notes you have marked, the weather, and the battery when it is charging or running low. You choose which of those appear, how many seconds each one gets, and what counts as leading — with apps and contacts that jump the queue.

Notifications

The newest message
Who wrote last and what they said, across the top of the screen, from any app that sends messages or only the ones you pick. One, two or three lines, gone after a while.
Notification dots
Marks dock icons that have something waiting.
Your own notes
Every note can also appear as a silent notification — no sound, no vibration, no badge — and Done on the notification deletes the note.

All three need notification access, which you grant in system settings and can take back there at any time.

The assistant bar

A line you type an errand into, and it does it: reminders, notes, messages, emails, calls, calendar entries, apps and searches. Begin a line with “todo”, “to do” or “later” and it asks when to remind you. “Clear Friday” opens that day with a way to delete each event.

Put events straight in
Skips the calendar app. Without permission to write, it opens the calendar with everything filled in instead.
Where notes go
A note widget on the page you are on, Google Keep, or both. Keep is on out of the box — see the privacy policy.
Pinned notes
A line with no time on it, which clears itself after as long as you say.
What it searches
Apps and contacts. Not your photos, music, files or messages — there is nothing else it is able to search.
When it cannot read a line
Without a key, it becomes a web search. Paste in a key for Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini and the line is sent there instead, with the date and time and nothing else.

Foldables

Unfolding gives Orbital a screen about twice as wide. You decide what it does with it:

Same layout
The folded screen's arrangement, stretched — either filling the width, or with two pages side by side so a swipe crosses both.
A layout of its own
The two screens part company: their own pages, their own widgets, and settings of their own. Orbital shows a picture of each, and either can be arranged from the settings whichever way the phone happens to be open.

With a layout of its own, syncing is turned off: a change made in the ordinary settings reaches only the screen in your hand. Turn syncing back on and a change reaches both — and says so, along the bottom, with an undo for the screen you are not looking at.

The dock gets the same treatment. A wheel sized for a thumb on a folded phone is a small arc adrift on an unfolded one, so the big screen can have its own size, edge and position.

Backups & starting over

Save a backup
Every setting, both screens' worth, your dock, your pages and your widgets, as one file you keep.
Save this layout to share
The arrangement without what is written on it, with a list of exactly what is going before it is written.
Save a full copy
Everything, notes and all — for moving to a new phone, not for handing to anybody.
Load a layout
Replaces your pages, widgets and dock. Orbital lists what goes first, and keeps one copy of what it replaced so you can put it back.
Restore defaults
Clears every setting, the docks, the home screen and what Orbital has learned. Your assistant key and saved backups stay.

Every gesture

Swipe up
The app drawer.
Swipe down
Nothing, the glance, or the assistant bar — your choice.
Swipe sideways
The page next door. Up and down too, where you have added one.
Hold the background
The home menu: widgets, edit mode, wallpaper, the clock, settings.
Pinch
Edit mode, if you would rather reach it that way.
Double tap the background
Turns the screen off, if you turn that on.
Turn the dock
Drag along it. Drag outward from an icon to pull the orbits behind it out.
Hold an app
Everything you can do with it: open, keep it where it is, hide it, rename it, its own shortcuts, uninstall.

This guide describes Orbital as it stands today. Settings are added and renamed as the launcher is built; the app's own settings search is always the last word.
